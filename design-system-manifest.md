# Design-system manifest — rendered UI design law

The agent-readable design law a builder reads before generating any UI, the way it
reads [AGENTS.md](./AGENTS.md). It holds only the law Huey has ruled, transcribed by
an agent; an agent records a ruling here and never invents one. Where the law is
silent, ask Huey before painting; the gap is not filled here.

The drawings are in [`lexema-design.pen`](./lexema-design.pen). Boards 08 to 22 are
the current design; frames 00 to 07 and their notes are the earlier design, kept for
history. Where a board and this file disagree, this file wins and the board is fixed.

## Settled law

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| Headings, section names, controls and links are in English. Grammar labels on a result are Italian: the part of speech, moods, tenses, persons, gender, number, and the non-finite labels. | [ADR 0015](./.decisions/0015-grammar-labels-are-italian.md) |
| Definitions, examples, and forms appear in the Italian the source wrote them in, never translated or paraphrased. | [ADR 0004](./.decisions/0004-cloudflare-workers-d1-vinext.md) |
| The result page shows data only. It adds no note explaining missing, unplaced, derived or disputed data: an empty slot is a dash in its cell or simply absent, a form the page cannot place in a grid or table is not shown, and a disputed claim is not marked on the page (the review stays in the data). Where there is no data, the page shows nothing. | [README.md](./README.md); ruled by Huey on 2026-09-27 |
| A lookup returns every candidate, and the interface may rank but never drops one from that answer. Which of them a word page shows as readings is ruled in [How a word page renders § 1](#1-what-becomes-a-reading). | [AGENTS.md](./AGENTS.md), the product rule; on the word page, Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06 on [#695](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/695#issuecomment-6024404411) |
| A generated short explanation may appear in Italian and in English, labelled as generated. Its example sentence, and everything from the source, stays Italian. | [ADR 0008](./.decisions/0008-generated-explanations-are-labelled-and-reportable.md), amendment of 2026-09-21 |
| One small *Source* link per result page, to the Wiktionary page of the spelling in its title; a searched expression's short page links to the expression's page; a page for a word only its forms name links the page of the first form its forms table shows; no credit line on the search page. The full credit is on `/licence`, linked from the footer. | [ADR 0009](./.decisions/0009-two-licences-and-a-source-link.md), amendments #281, #459 and #139 |

## The page

Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-21: "put the search bar in the middle of the screen
first and then when I search, it goes top as right now."

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Two states.** | Before a query, the page is the search bar alone, centred on the screen with the site name above it and nothing else competing. Under the name, centred, sit its pronunciation `/lekˈsɛːma/` in mono, muted, as a word's pronunciation is drawn, and *a simple dictionary* in italic serif (board 00). With a query, the bar sits at the top and the results fill the page below it. |
| **One bar, one route.** | The same form serves both states; the query stays in the URL so a result can be shared. Moving the bar is layout, not a second page. The `×` in the bar empties it and keeps the cursor there; the result stays until a new search is sent. ⌘K on a Mac, Ctrl+K elsewhere, scrolls to the top (smoothly, or at once for a reader who asks for reduced motion) and puts the cursor in the bar with its text selected, on the home page and on a result; not while a dialog is open or the reader is typing in another field. While the bar does not have the cursor, a small muted `⌘K` (`Ctrl K`) sits where the `ENTER` hint does, left of the `×` when there is text; a touch-only screen does not show it. |
| **Suggestions include expressions.** | While a reader types several words, the list under the bar also offers what they are typing completed as an expression, in their own words: `andare v` offers *andare via*, `vado v` offers *vado via*. It is offered only when it finds a stored multi-word headword, after the words that begin with what was typed. Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-30 ([#214](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/214#issuecomment-5907869562)). |

## The result

Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-27, on boards 08 to 21. These rules replace the result-card
rules of 2026-09-21 (boxed paradigms, header bars, three boxes to a row).

### Layout

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **One layout for every word type.** | Headword, then its readings in source order, then the facts about the word, then *Source*. No box or card surrounds a reading; readings are separated by a thin rule. |
| **The headword carries its pronunciation.** | The IPA sits under the headword. Syllable breaks are not shown. A word with no pronunciation shows none. |
| **Jump links for three or more readings, or for two readings about two different words.** | Ruled in [How a word page renders § 2](#2-numbers-and-jump-links). |
| **A reading is a heading and blocks.** | Ruled in [How a word page renders § 2 to § 4](#how-a-word-page-renders). |
| **Section labels are plain.** | A small grey label (*Definitions*, *Forms*, *Etymology*, *Synonyms*). No count, no rule beside it, no box around the block. |
| **One expand control, no count.** | Closed, a block shows its first part, then a small accent `+ more` right at the end of what shows. Open, it shows everything, then `less` at the very end. No control carries a count. It works with no script: the whole content is in the HTML. *Etymology*: one line cut with an ellipsis, `+ more` right after it; open, `less` follows the text's last word; a text that fits on its line has no control. *Synonyms*, *Antonyms*, *Derived words* and the not-found offers: the words that fit on one line, `+ more` after the last of them on that line; open, every word, then `less`; words that all fit have no control. *Definitions*: the first definition and its own first example, or none if it has none of its own (no other definition's example stands in), then `+ more` under it; open, every definition with all its examples in order, then `less`. Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-27. |
| **Word facts come once, after the readings, unless the source ties them to a reading.** | *Etymology* as prose, one line, in every Etymology block, in a reading or after the readings, on a phone too. *Synonyms* as a run of words separated by `·`. Both open with the one expand control. On a word with two readings or more, an etymology whose opening bracket names a part of speech (`sale`'s `(sostantivo singolare) derivato dal greco …`) shows in that reading, after *Forms*, without the bracket; a synonym group the source opens with a part-of-speech label (`sostantivo`, `verbo`) shows in that reading in the same style. Only a label that names exactly one reading moves its text; a label that names two, whether two parts (`medico`'s `(aggettivo e sostantivo)`) or two readings of one kind (`svolta`'s two *Voce verbale* readings), is never copied into both. Whatever names no single reading (such a label, a topic label such as `(sport)`, an unlabelled text or list) stays once after the readings, with no note. A text that is only its label leaves no block. Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-27. A text whose label names only forms, and every word fact of a form, show nowhere: [How a word page renders § 5](#5-word-facts-on-a-form-page). |
| **Source and report sit together at the bottom.** | `Source ↗ · Report a mistake`, with the same space above it and at least that space below it; on a page shorter than the window, the footer sits at the bottom of the window and the extra room falls between Source and the footer. Exactly one *Source*, with no word after it, to the Wiktionary page of the spelling in the title: `macchina` links to *macchina*'s page, which holds both its readings, and `andavano` to *andavano*'s, though it shows `andare`'s table. A searched expression's short page links to the expression's page instead. Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-30 ([#281](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/281)). On a page for a word only its forms name, *Source* opens the page of the first form its forms table shows, in the order the table renders and skipping the title spelling a grid puts in its own cell: `verbalizzare` links to *verbalizzando*'s page, `fratellino` to *fratellini*'s. That is where the shown data comes from, and the title word almost never has a page. Ruled by Huey on 2026-10-03 ([#459](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/459#issuecomment-5969625294)). What the report box asks is on board 22 and in [#51](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/51). |

### Forms: three shapes

A reading's *Forms* block takes the shape its data has. Nothing else about the
reading changes with the word type.

| Shape | Used for | What it means on the page |
|---|---|---|
| **None** | adverbs, proper names, abbreviations, prefixes and suffixes, grammar words, most phrases | No *Forms* block. |
| **Gender × number grid** | nouns, adjectives, phrases that inflect | Columns *singolare* and *plurale*; rows *maschile* and *femminile*, only the rows the source has. Each cell is the form, with its definite and indefinite article under it (`il bello · un bello`; on a phone, on two lines). An adjective's superlative is a second grid of the same shape, labelled *superlativo*. |
| **Conjugation** | verbs, and the lemma of a searched verb form | One line of non-finite forms (*gerundio*, *participio*, *ausiliare*). Then mood tabs (*Indicativo*, *Congiuntivo*, *Condizionale*, *Imperativo*), then a table with persons down and the simple tenses across, named in Italian. The compound tenses wait behind the one `+ more` right after that table; opened, *Tempi semplici* and *Tempi composti* are named and `less` ends them. One open state serves every mood tab of a table: `+ more` on one tab opens the compound tenses on all of them, and `less` on any closes them on all (Huey, 2026-10-06, [#683](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/683)). The tabs open on the mood of the searched form, else *Indicativo*. In a compound tense, a spelling built on essere shows both genders, `sono andato/a` on a singular row and `siamo andati/e` on a plural one, by rule `it-essere-agreement/v1`; it still links to the source's spelling. A reflexive verb's cells do the same (`mi sono accorto/a`, `ci siamo accorti/e`), and its simple tenses stay as given (`mi accorgo`). In a cell of several spellings only the first agrees, and the rest stay exactly as stored: `mi sono arreso/a, arresosi`, `sono assorbito/a, assorto`. An avere spelling stays as the source gives it, and the non-finite line stays masculine (`participio andato`). Ruled by Huey on 2026-10-06 ([#676](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/676), [table shape](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/676#issuecomment-6013478337)). A cell whose spellings are built on two auxiliaries puts each auxiliary's spellings on a line of their own, with no comma or dot between the lines, by rule `it-compound-auxiliary/v1`: vivere's `io` cell reads `ho vissuto` / `sono vissuto/a`, assorbire's `ho assorbito, assorto` / `sono assorbito/a, assorto`. A cell on one auxiliary stays one line, and a two-line cell makes its whole row taller in both paired tables. On a phone a tense cell keeps the 16 px gap the heads draw before the next column, and a form wraps only at a space, never inside a word (`ti sei` / `accorto/a`). Ruled by Huey on 2026-10-06 ([#683](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/683)). |

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **A searched form is marked only in a conjugation.** | In a verb table the searched form is underlined in the accent colour, and its person and tense labels take the accent. A grid does not mark it; it is obvious there. Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-23 ([#111](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/111)): marking stays with verbs, never a noun or adjective box. |
| **A searched form leads to its lemma's table.** | Ruled in [How a word page renders § 4](#4-a-forms-block). |
| **A searched expression gets a short page.** | Ruled in [How a word page renders § 6](#6-other-page-kinds). |
| **Forms and synonyms are links.** | Every form in a conjugation table and every synonym links to its own search. No underline and no hover colour; the pointer cursor is the only cue. A cell holding several spellings links each one. |
| **Groups follow the source's own vocabulary.** | Moods, then tenses, in the order the tag vocabulary lists them. A form that fits no cell, such as `mangiarsi (coniugazione)`, is not shown, and nothing says so. |

### On a phone

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Same order, re-stacked.** | Boards 19 to 21. Nothing the wide page shows is dropped, and nothing scrolls sideways. |
| **Grids stack by gender.** | The gender label sits above each singular and plural pair. |
| **Conjugations show two tenses at a time.** | The four mood tabs stay on one line. The table shows two tenses side by side, then the next two below. The non-finite forms are three short rows. |
| **Synonyms stay one line until opened.** | Closed, the words that fit on the phone's line; open, the run wraps onto as many lines as it needs. |

## How a word page renders

The one home for which readings a word page shows and how each reads. A row
elsewhere in this file that touches these rules links here and does not repeat
them. It starts from the audit of 59 live words on
[#695](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/695#issuecomment-6024240627),
whose problem numbers (P1 to P14) it cites.

Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06 on #695
([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/695#issuecomment-6024404411)),
"yes" to four rules, covering P1 and P3 to P11:

> 1. **One block per base word.** A form page shows one block per word it is a form of: form lines, then that word's Definitions, then its table. Never two blocks or two tables for the same word (stato, vivere, costruttrici).
> 2. **Only records about the searched word.** Sibling forms and superlatives of other words never show (grandissimo on grande, costruttore's copies on costruttrici).
> 3. **No base-word extras on any form page.** No "vedi …" etymology, no expressions or synonyms of the base word — for noun/adjective forms too, not only verbs (bella, vada).
> 4. **Same layout everywhere.** Forms with no record of their own (gravida, citta) and expression pages (vado via) use the same form-line layout.

All four are law. #695 built rules 1 and 2, and
[#700](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/700) rules 3 and 4. P2, a page
where nothing at all is left to show, is ruled on
[#696](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/696) ([§ 1](#1-what-becomes-a-reading)).

Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on #708
([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6047197445),
"all yes") answers the shapes the census of every word page found with no rule
([#707](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/707)). Each question's
recommended answer is the rule: questions 2 to 6
([asked here](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6037356232))
and 7 to 9 ([asked here](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6037557199))
are stated in the rows below.

### 1. What becomes a reading

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Only records about the searched word.** | A record is a reading only when it is about the word searched: its own headword is that word. A record that only lists the word in its forms table is another word's, and never a reading of its own: sibling forms and lemma records (`costruttrici` shows none of costruttore's or costruttori's records, `bellissima` not bella's), and superlatives (`grande` and `grandi` do not show `grandissimo`, whose only tie is its gloss "superlativo assoluto di grande"). Such a record shows only as the verb of a block (§ 4). The lookup still returns it, and its own word's search still shows it. Rule 2; it settles P1 and P10, and replaces, for the word page, the product rule that every candidate is rendered and the exception that kept a lemma's page free of its forms (Huey, 2026-10-05, [#622](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/622); "bella is the same as bello", [#626](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/626)). |
| **A record whose headword differs only in capitals is a reading.** | Search ignores capitals, so a record whose own headword is the searched word in other capitals is about the word searched, and is a reading, as today: `abaco` shows `Abaco` (Nome proprio), and `AC` shows `ac`. A reader who types `abaco` may mean the name. Question 2 of Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on #708 ([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6047197445)). |
| **A reading with nothing to show is not rendered.** | No definition or example, no form line, no table of its own or of its lemma, no etymology and no synonym: the reading is left out, and nothing on the page says so. `litigante` reads `1 · Aggettivo · maschile e femminile, singolare` then `2 · Voce verbale`, its noun, whose one sense has no gloss, left out. Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06 ([#694](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/694), "yes fix 1 and 2"). |
| **A page where every reading is empty (P2) keeps the part of speech under the word.** | Such a page keeps its readings' headings, and the word's own facts after them (pronunciation, etymology, synonyms) as on any page. When it has only one reading, the heading is the part of speech alone, with no number and no leading dot: `sbucciapatate` reads `Sostantivo`, `fare l'abitudine` reads `Locuzione verbale` then *Synonyms*. Two or more keep their numbers, `1 · Sostantivo`, `2 · …`. Nothing on the page says a definition is missing; it shows only what the source page has. Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06 on [#696](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/696#issuecomment-6024433065) (step 3, "without the '1 ·' when it is the only reading"; he asked what Wiktionary does). Where the word has no record of its own with anything to show, the readings are the records that list it, less the forms of its readings and of its lemma (#622, #626), as before #695. Among those records, a noun's or adjective's whose grid spells the word is no reading but a form block (rule 4, P3; [§ 4](#4-a-forms-block), "A form no record describes"): `gravida` and `citta` show one. |

### 2. Numbers and jump links

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Every reading is numbered, and the numbers never skip.** | Every reading and block the page shows is numbered 1, 2, 3 in page order, with or without a definition: `salivare` reads `1 · Aggettivo · maschile e femminile, singolare` then `2 · Verbo`, its *Forms* under it. A reading with no definition has no *Definitions* block. Huey's direction of 2026-10-06 ([#687](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/687)), reversing his ruling of 2026-09-30 on [#250](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/250). A reading left out (§ 1) takes no number. The one exception is a page whose only reading is empty, which shows its part of speech with no number (§ 1, [#696](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/696)). The report dialog lists the readings the page shows, with the same numbers, and names such a lone reading by its part of speech alone. |
| **Jump links for three or more readings, or for two readings about two different words.** | Under the headword, one link per reading the page shows, `1 Aggettivo`, each with its number, a reading with no definition included ([#687](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/687)). A reading left out has no link, and the count leaves it out ([#694](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/694)). A verb form block is its heading's words, `3 Voce verbale · salire`, and points to its own anchor ([#636](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/636)). A page with three or more readings always has them, except an expression page, which never has them: `Venerdì santi` and `servizio sanitario nazionali` show three readings and no list ([§ 6](#6-other-page-kinds); question 6 of Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on #708, [ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6047197445)). A page with two has them when its two readings are about two different words: a reading of the word's own record is about its own headword, and a form's block or reading about the word it is a form of. `studente` shows `1 Sostantivo` and `2 Voce verbale · studiare`, the noun and a form of studiare (Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on [#708](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6036050200), "yes i like your recommendation"; [#714](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/714)), and `salivate` shows `1 Voce verbale · salire` `2 Voce verbale · salivare` (frame 37; Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06, "if the verb is different we have to write at the top, that's good"; [#654](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/654)). Two readings of one word have none: `salivare`'s `1 · Aggettivo` and `2 · Verbo`, and `andare`'s noun and verb. A page with one reading has none. |
| **Headwords that differ only in capitals are two words.** | For the jump links, two headwords that differ only in capitals are two different words, as today, the same reason as `studente`: they are two Wiktionary pages. `abaco` lists `1 Sostantivo` `2 Nome proprio`, its noun and `Abaco`'s; so do `AC`, `ADA` and `ATM`. Question 3 of Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on #708 ([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6047197445)). |
| **Headwords that differ only in their apostrophe are one word.** | Two headwords that differ only in which apostrophe they use, `'`, `’`, `‘` or `ʼ` (the four `normalizeItalianExact` in `src/italian/normalize.ts` folds into one), are one word, as search already treats them. So a page with two readings about them has no jump links: `all'improvviso`, `fare l'occhiolino` and `in un batter d'occhio`. Question 4 of Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on #708 ([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/708#issuecomment-6047197445)). |

### 3. A reading of a word's own record

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **A heading, then Definitions, then Forms.** | The heading is `1 · Sostantivo`: the number, then the record's `pos_title`. A reading that takes a gender × number grid adds, muted, the gender and number its record states, in Italian: `1 · Aggettivo · maschile, singolare`, `2 · Sostantivo, forma flessa · femminile, plurale`; both numbers when it states both (`khmer`: `maschile, singolare e plurale`), only the one it states when it states one, and nothing when it states neither. Verbs, *Voce verbale*, proper names and other readings without a grid add nothing. On a phone the heading wraps. Then *Definitions*, the first shown and the rest behind `+ more`, then *Forms* in [its shape](#forms-three-shapes). A block with nothing to show is left out. A proper name and a phrase read the same way (frames 14 and 15). |
| **A table shows once on a page.** | When two readings would draw the same table, only the first draws it, and the second keeps its own *Definitions*: `essere` and `vivere`, each two `Verbo` records of one verb, draw the conjugation once, under `2 · Verbo`. Rule 1, "never two … tables for the same word"; it settles P7. |

### 4. A form's block

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **One block per base word.** | A form shows one block per word it is a form of: its form lines, then that word's *Definitions*, then that word's table (rule 1). A word that is itself a form leads on to the word it is a form of, the base word: `bellissima`, "femminile di bellissimo", shows bello's *Definitions* and *Forms of bello*, and `costruttrici`, "plurale di costruttrice", costruttore's (P1). The form's records of one base word are one block, whatever their part of speech: `costruttrici`'s adjective and noun records, `bella`'s and `grandi`'s, each show as one block, with the first record's lines and then the next record's, an identical line once (`costruttrici`'s two "plurale di costruttrice" are one). Its heading names every part of speech of those records, in record order, each once, and its *Definitions* hold the base word's meanings for each of them, as [A noun or adjective form block](#4-a-forms-block) rules (Huey's ruling of 2026-10-07 on [#727](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/727)). A base word's table shows once on the page. A form-of reading never shows a *Forms* table of its own, with its base word's table or without (Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06, "2 is too much repetitive", [#694](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/694)). Nothing marks how a block was put together. |
| **A verb form block.** | One block per verb a searched form belongs to, as frame 37 draws it ([#636](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/636), ruled by Huey on 2026-10-06: "if the verb is different we have to write at the top"; "'sono andato' needs to be one block"). The block is headed `N · Voce verbale · <verb>`: the source's own heading for a form, then the verb, in the heading's own weight, the dot muted. A verb that has a block has no `Verbo` reading. A searched word that is also an entry of its own (`sale` the noun, `andare` itself) keeps that entry as a normal reading. `stato`, whose only cell in essere's table is its participio, shows one `Voce verbale · essere` block, not essere's two `Verbo` readings (P8). Searching `salivate` shows `1 · Voce verbale · salire` and `2 · Voce verbale · salivare`, each line under its own verb's heading and above its own verb's table. |
| **A noun or adjective form block.** | A noun or adjective form keeps its own heading, with its gender and number: searching `bellissima` shows `1 · Aggettivo, forma flessa · femminile, singolare`, its line ("femminile di bellissimo", linked to `bellissimo`), then *Definitions*, bello's own as bello's page lists them, then *Forms of bello*, bello's grid in place of bellissima's own, with nothing marked ([#686](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/686), revising ruling A of #626). A block whose records have two parts of speech or more is headed by each of them, without `forma flessa`, then the first record's gender and number: `bella`'s adjective record and its noun record, `Sostantivo, forma flessa`, both forms of bello, are one block (rule 1) headed `1 · Aggettivo · Sostantivo · femminile, singolare`. Its lines follow, "femminile singolare di bello" then "femminile di bello"; then *Definitions*, one group per part of speech in the heading's order, each under a small label naming it: *Aggettivo* over bello's adjective meanings, *Sostantivo* over the meanings of bello's two noun records; then one *Forms of bello*. `costruttrici` shows "che costruisce" under *Aggettivo* and "chi costruisce" under *Sostantivo*. Closed, the first group's label and its first meaning show; the groups share the block's one `+ more`, and no meaning shows twice. A block whose records share one part of speech (`blasfeme`'s two adjective records) keeps its first record's heading and has no group label. A verb form record whose lines join the block (the row below) adds no part of speech. ### 5. Word facts on a form page

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **No base-word extras on any page (P5, P6).** | The word's Etymology, Synonyms, Antonyms and Derived words are those of its own records that are not forms. What a form record says of its word is its base word's, and shows nowhere: no "vedi …" or "da …" etymology, no synonym, antonym or derived word, for noun and adjective forms as for verbs (rule 3). `bella`, `belli`, `belle`, `studenti`, `grandi`, `attrici`, `lavoratrici` and `costruttrici` show no `vedi <lemma>`; `belli` none of its inflected synonyms; `andassi` and `vada` no `da andare`, and `vada` no `si · muova`. A text the source labels with only forms shows nowhere either ([§ 4](#4-a-forms-block), P14). No page shows a base word's *Expressions*: there is no *Expressions with <word>* (`bella`, `case`, `grandi`, `parti`). Rule 3 of Huey's ruling of 2026-10-06; built by #700. It covers #668's rulings 1 and 2, a verb shown as a block bringing no Expressions and a form page showing no `vedi <verb>` ([ruling 1](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/668#issuecomment-6012830482), [ruling 2](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/668#issuecomment-6013026186)). |
| **A word's own facts stay.** | A page with a reading of its own that is not a form keeps the word's own Etymology, Synonyms, Antonyms and *Expressions*: `andata`, with `andata` the noun, keeps `vedi andare`, its synonyms and `ritorno`; `sale` the noun keeps `derivato dal greco …`. The expressions a form's own records list are the word's too: `andate` keeps its own *Expressions* (ruling 3, [#668](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/668#issuecomment-6013035148)). Ruled by Huey on 2026-10-06; this overrides frame 17x. |

## When a search finds nothing

Ruled by Huey on 2026-09-27, board 24.

| Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Say it, then offer what is close.** | The heading is `No entry for "<query>"`, serif, large, with the page's own search bar above it and the footer at the foot of the window. Under it, the first of these that finds something: the same letters with an accent (`Did you mean città?`, the word in the accent colour, then *Other words that begin with "citta"*); a spelling one edit away (`Did you mean mangiare?`, then *Other close spellings*; among spellings equally close, the word translated into the most languages leads, so `mangare` offers `mangiare` before `magnare`); a search of several words corrected so that it reads as an expression, kept as typed, with one word misspelled, the last word unfinished, or only part of it an expression (`Did you mean vado via?` for "vadoo via", `Did you mean tiro fuori?` for "tiro fuory", then *Other expressions*; each opens that search's short page); the words that begin with what was typed (*Suggestions*, one line, then `+ more`); or, with nothing close, "Lexema has no word spelled this way. Check the spelling, or search for the word's base form: the infinitive of a verb, the singular of a noun." A search that finds a word still offers it with an accent or a final apostrophe when a headword writes it so (`citta` finds `citto`'s form and offers `città`; `e` offers `è`, `po` offers `po'`), never a spelling that drops a mark the query has. It is one line right under the search bar, above the result, which shows unchanged below it: `Did you mean città?` in the not-found offer's form, smaller, as board 32 draws it (*Did you mean* in small muted sans, the first ranked word in the accent colour in serif, linked to its search). Ruled by Huey on 2026-10-03 ([ruling](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/478#issuecomment-5969664345), [board 32](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/issues/478#issuecomment-5969753067)). |

## Role tokens: the dark scheme

The nine colour roles take the values of the design file's `variables`, moved
into the token layer by [#102](https://github.com/povlabs/lexema/pull/102). They
land as oklch in the `@theme` block of `web/app/globals.css`. A component reaches
for a role, never a value.

| Role | Design file | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `surface` | `#121110` | the page background |
| `surface-raised` | `#1A1917` | the search bar, the report box |
| `border` | `#2C2A26` | thin rules between readings and sections |
| `border-strong` | `#4E4940` | control outlines |
| `text-muted` | `#8B8579` | labels, grammar labels, articles, examples |
| `text` | `#C4BEB2` | body prose, etymology |
| `text-strong` | `#F4F0E6` | the headword, definitions, forms, synonyms |
| `accent` | `#D2A85C` | reading numbers, links, the `+ more` control, the searched form in a conjugation |
| `warning` | `#D9704F` | no use on the result page, which marks no dispute |

When a colour changes in Pencil, re-export the design file's variables to
[`web/test/fixtures/design-variables.json`](web/test/fixtures/design-variables.json);
`web/test/tokens.test.ts` fails until the stylesheet matches that snapshot.

Three families, served from this repository: **Spectral** for the headword,
definitions, examples, etymology and synonyms; **Inter** for labels, controls and
grammar labels; **IBM Plex Mono** for forms, articles and pronunciation. The dark
scheme is the only scheme.
